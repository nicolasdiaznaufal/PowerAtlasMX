    Nodal solar value · CENACE MDA · SIN

    What is a solar MWh worth at each node?

    We value a 1 MWp photovoltaic plant at every node of the national grid by multiplying its hour-by-hour production by the hour-by-hour nodal price, and compare that with the simple average price used in quick prospection. Then we ask which nodes cover their cost.

    About the project

    What this is, what it uses, what it answers

    Nodal Solar Value is an origination tool for REPSA. It turns three public data sources into one number per node: the price a solar plant would actually capture in the day-ahead market.

    The project

    One plant, every node, every hour

    We place a reference plant of 1 MWp at each of the ~2,650 nodes of the CENACE market, model its hourly output, and price every MWh at the node's own hourly price. The result is a captured price and a value factor for each node, state and control region.

    Goals

    Screen with the captured price

    Replace the average-price shortcut with a number that reflects when the sun shines and what the grid pays at that moment. Find where solar keeps its value, where it loses it, and whether a node can cover a given cost per MWh.

    Why it matters

    Midday is cheap and getting cheaper

    Prices fall when many plants produce at once. A node that looks good on average prices can be mediocre on the hours a plant really sells. The gap is large, it differs by region, and it is growing.

    Tools and data

    Prices

    CENACE PML, day-ahead market (MDA)

    Hourly locational marginal prices (PML, in MXN/MWh) for about 2,650 nodes, 2020 to Sep 2026. Each price is energy + losses + congestion. This is the reference market price, not a plant's full revenue.

    Weather

    NASA POWER hourly weather

    Hourly sunlight (GHI, DNI, DHI), air temperature and wind on a 1° grid, 2015 to 2025. Each node uses the cell of its municipality. Fine for screening, not for financing.

    Production

    pvlib PV model

    Open-source Python library. It turns each hour of weather into energy per kWp for a single-axis tracker and a fixed-tilt plant. Details in the appendix.

    The pipeline

    1. 1 · IngestCENACE PML files and NASA POWER weather are downloaded and cached.
    2. 2 · CDMX clockEvery hour of every system is put on one fixed clock (CDMX standard time, UTC−6).
    3. 3 · QualityGaps, duplicates, short histories and renamed nodes are flagged; nothing is silently dropped.
    4. 4 · Additive aggregatesSums by node, month and hour, so any period can be recombined in the browser.
    5. 5 · KPIsCaptured price, value factor, share of energy at PML ≤ 0, revenue per MWp, today's pesos.
    6. 6 · Independent validationA second implementation recomputes the KPIs; additive sums match to the cent.
    Questions it answers
    • What is a MWh of solar worth at this node, in pesos and as a share of the average price?
    • How much does the average-price shortcut overstate revenue, and where?
    • Is value falling over time, and how fast?
    • Which nodes, states and regions cover a given cost per MWh, and by how much?
    Scope and limits
    • Reference price only. The captured price uses the MDA PML. It excludes settlement of imbalances, capacity (potencia), CELs, ancillary services, transmission charges and bilateral contracts.
    • Typical-year production. Output uses a 2015–2025 typical year applied to each real hour of price.
    • Node = municipal centroid. Locations are not substations; see the Geography block.
    • REPSA assumptions are pending. Capex, O&M and rate come from you, in the Assumptions panel.
    01 · Notation · read this once

    Every symbol on this page

    Four families: what the market pays, what the plant produces, how the panel behaves, and what it costs. Each later section repeats the symbols it uses.

    Price

    \(h\) hour of the day, 0–23, hour in which the interval starts. CENACE publishes 1–24 (hour ending), so \(h=\text{hora}-1\).

    \(\mathrm{PML}_h\) day-ahead nodal price in hour \(h\), MXN/MWh = energy \(\lambda^E\) + losses \(\lambda^L\) + congestion \(\lambda^C\).

    \(N\) number of hours in the period (8,760 in a year).

    \(\overline{\mathrm{PML}}\) simple average of all \(N\) hourly prices.

    \(P_{\mathrm{cap}}\) captured price: production-weighted average price.

    \(\mathrm{VF}\) value factor, \(P_{\mathrm{cap}}/\overline{\mathrm{PML}}\), no unit.

    Production

    \(P_{\mathrm{STC}}\) nameplate DC power of the panels at standard test conditions, kWp or MWp.

    \(E_h\) AC energy delivered in hour \(h\), MWh.

    \(y_h\) energy per installed kWp in hour \(h\), kWh/kWp (= MWh/MWp).

    \(Y_f\) annual yield \(\sum_h y_h\), kWh/kWp.

    \(\mathrm{GHI}\) sunlight on a horizontal surface, W/m².

    \(G_{\mathrm{POA}}\) sunlight on the tilted panel surface (plane of array), W/m².

    Panel physics

    \(G_{\mathrm{STC}},T_{\mathrm{STC}}\) test reference: 1,000 W/m² and 25 °C cell temperature.

    \(T_a,\ T_c,\ v\) air temperature, cell temperature (°C), wind speed (m/s).

    \(\gamma\) power temperature coefficient of the panel, %/°C, negative.

    \(L_{\mathrm{sys}},\ \eta_{\mathrm{inv}}\) system losses (wiring, soiling, mismatch); inverter efficiency.

    \(r\) DC/AC ratio: panel kWp per kW of inverter.

    \(\mathrm{PR}\) performance ratio: share of plane-of-array sunlight that leaves the plant as AC energy.

    Economics

    \(R\) revenue from selling energy at the node, MXN.

    \(R_{\mathrm{short}}\) revenue as if every MWh sold at \(\overline{\mathrm{PML}}\) (the average-price shortcut).

    \(H_{\mathrm{GHI}}\) annual horizontal irradiation, kWh/m².

    \(\mathrm{LCOE}\) levelized cost of energy, USD/MWh (or MXN/MWh).

    \(\mathrm{VF}_{\mathrm{req}}\) value factor needed to break even.

    \(\Pi\) annual margin: revenue minus levelized cost, MXN.

    Units: prices in MXN/MWh as published by CENACE. "Module" and "panel" mean the same thing: one photovoltaic panel. USD figures use the exchange rate you set in Assumptions (the deck scenario uses 17.84 MXN/USD).

    02 · Prices · simple average vs captured price

    A plant earns the captured price, not the average

    Both average the same hourly prices. Only the weights differ: equal for every hour, or proportional to what the plant produces.

    What a PML is · precio marginal local \[ \mathrm{PML}_{n,h}=\underbrace{\lambda^{E}_{h}}_{\text{system energy}}+\underbrace{\lambda^{L}_{n,h}}_{\text{losses}}+\underbrace{\lambda^{C}_{n,h}}_{\text{congestion}} \]

    Cost of serving one more MWh at node \(n\) in hour \(h\). System energy: cheapest generator if the grid had no limits; it changes every hour but is one value for the whole SIN (computed from the data). Losses and congestion differ by node: congestion averaged — MXN/MWh in Peninsular (cheap power cannot get in) and — in Noroeste (too much local supply to export).

    Simple average · every hour weighs 1/N\[ \overline{\mathrm{PML}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{h=1}^{N}\mathrm{PML}_h \]
    Captured price · every hour weighs by production\[ P_{\mathrm{cap}}=\sum_{h=1}^{N} w_h\,\mathrm{PML}_h,\qquad w_h=\frac{y_h}{\sum_{k=1}^{N} y_k},\qquad \sum_h w_h = 1 \]

    Night hours count in \(\overline{\mathrm{PML}}\) but have \(w_h=0\) in \(P_{\mathrm{cap}}\). If production and price were unrelated, both would be equal: \(P_{\mathrm{cap}}-\overline{\mathrm{PML}}=N\,\mathrm{Cov}(w,\mathrm{PML})\). In Mexico prices fall at midday, exactly when \(w_h\) is largest, so \(P_{\mathrm{cap}}<\overline{\mathrm{PML}}\).

    03 · Revenue · production × price, hour by hour

    What the value factor costs a plant

    Revenue is energy times the captured price. The average-price shortcut prices that energy at the simple average, i.e. it assumes VF = 1, and so overstates revenue by 1/VF − 1.

    Revenue of a plant at one node\[ R=\sum_h E_h\,\mathrm{PML}_h=P_{\mathrm{STC}}\sum_h y_h\,\mathrm{PML}_h = P_{\mathrm{STC}}\,Y_f\,P_{\mathrm{cap}} \]
    The average-price shortcut\[ R_{\mathrm{short}}=P_{\mathrm{STC}}\,Y_f\,\overline{\mathrm{PML}} \]
    Value factor\[ \mathrm{VF}=\frac{P_{\mathrm{cap}}}{\overline{\mathrm{PML}}}=\frac{R}{R_{\mathrm{short}}}\qquad \frac{R_{\mathrm{short}}}{R}-1=\frac{1}{\mathrm{VF}}-1 \]
    How to read the revenue chart

    Two bars per hour: what 1 MWp earns in that hour of an average day. Grey: the shortcut (energy × average price). Orange: what the node pays (energy × that hour's price). Orange shorter means the plant sells below the average price; summed over the day, the gap is the revenue the shortcut overstates.

    04 · Value factor · Hirth (2013)

    A falling VF is the fingerprint of cannibalization

    VF below 1 is normal in Mexico because demand peaks after sunset. Its level today matters less than two things: where it is heading, and the price level it multiplies.

    Definition · Hirth (2013) [4] \[ \mathrm{VF}=\frac{P_{\mathrm{cap}}}{\overline{\mathrm{PML}}}=\frac{\sum_h y_h\,\mathrm{PML}_h}{\overline{\mathrm{PML}}\,\sum_h y_h} \]

    VF = 1: the plant's hours are worth as much as the average hour. VF = 0.80: it collects 20 % less than the average price suggests.

    Why the trend and the price level matter more

    Trend. Every new solar plant adds energy in the same midday hours and pushes those prices down, so VF falls as solar grows. A plant lives 25–30 years: a VF that falls every year means future revenue below today's.

    Price level. Revenue per MWh is \(P_{\mathrm{cap}}=\mathrm{VF}\cdot\overline{\mathrm{PML}}\). Solar's VF rarely exceeds 1, so if \(\overline{\mathrm{PML}}\) itself is below the cost per MWh, no timing can rescue the node.

    Profitability test\[ P_{\mathrm{cap}}\ge \mathrm{LCOE}\iff \mathrm{VF}\ge\mathrm{VF}_{\mathrm{req}}=\frac{\mathrm{LCOE}}{\overline{\mathrm{PML}}} \]
    Two different ways solar loses value
    CurtailmentPrice cannibalization
    NaturePhysical limit on outputMarket effect on the price
    CauseGrid congestion, missing transmission, low demandMany PV plants producing in the same hours
    What happensOperator orders the plant to reduce outputPlant keeps producing, sells cheap or below zero
    LossMWh not producedMXN per MWh (lower VF)
    RemedyTransmission, storageStorage, PPA, site diversification

    Where they meet: at PML ≤ 0 a merchant plant prefers to curtail itself.

    05 · Profitability · merchant plant, energy revenue only

    Does a merchant plant pay for itself?

    A plant pays for itself when every MWh it sells earns more than it costs to produce. That cost per MWh is the LCOE. Every figure below recomputes from the assumption set you choose.

    LCOE in words

    The levelized cost of energy is the constant price per MWh at which a plant, over its whole life, recovers its investment, its operating costs and the return its investors require.

    Why it matters. It turns all project costs into the market's unit, MXN/MWh, so it can be compared directly with the captured price. It is the hurdle every MWh must clear: above it the plant creates value, below it the plant destroys value however much it produces.

    LCOE, formally\[ \mathrm{LCOE}=\frac{\sum_{t=0}^{T}(\mathrm{CAPEX}_t+\mathrm{O\&M}_t)\,(1+i)^{-t}}{\sum_{t=1}^{T}E_t\,(1+i)^{-t}} \]
    Break-even condition\[ P_{\mathrm{cap}}\ge\mathrm{LCOE}\iff\mathrm{VF}\ge\mathrm{VF}_{\mathrm{req}}=\frac{\mathrm{LCOE}}{\overline{\mathrm{PML}}} \]
    06 · Node P&L · one plant, one place, one year

    One plant at one node: does it make money?

    Margin is energy times the gap between captured price and LCOE. Plant size and yield only scale the result; whether it is a profit or a loss depends on the captured price versus the cost.

    Energy and revenue\[ E=P_{\mathrm{STC}}\,Y_f,\qquad R=E\,P_{\mathrm{cap}}=E\cdot\mathrm{VF}\cdot\overline{\mathrm{PML}} \]
    Cost and margin\[ C=E\cdot\mathrm{LCOE},\qquad \Pi=R-C=E\,\bigl(P_{\mathrm{cap}}-\mathrm{LCOE}\bigr) \]

    LCOE already includes capex, O&M and the investors' return, so \(\Pi\) is the value created above that return, not an accounting profit.
